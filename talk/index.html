I am Kasra Sinaei from Penn State, presenting our work on safe adaptive control with vanishing conservativeness. Adaptive controllers are a well established control practice, and engineers have used them for over four decades. Control Barrier Functions are also a well established, popular choice for safety filtering.
The Problem

Enforcing safety while the model estimate is not accurate

An adaptive controller is least informed at t = 0, exactly when the robot starts moving.

The filter needs the model you are still learning: with poor estimates it can fail to prevent unsafe behavior, or stay conservative forever.

\(\breve W=\hat W+\delta W\) \(\breve W\) Control law \(u\) Robot

A real-time QP perturbs the parameter estimate instead, carrying a model-error bound that shrinks as the robot learns. No external filter.

Model-based safety filters are most vulnerable at the beginning of the roll-out, and need to be conservative.
What makes integrating CBFs and adaptive control difficult is that a CBF needs a model, and adaptive control does not have an accurate system representation when it starts rolling out at time zero. To address this challenge, we propose filtering the parameter gains of the adaptive controller instead of the final action of the controller.
Problem Formulation  ·  Sec. II & III

Regressor-based representation of a nonlinear system

System \[ \dot{x} \;=\; f(x) \;+\; g(x)\,u \] (1)
A1 \(f\) and \(g\) fully unknown: we know only their dimensions. Fully actuated.
Goal Track \(x_d(t)\) and keep \(\mathcal C=\{x:h(x)\ge0\}\) forward invariant, with no model.
Everything unknown, in one vector \[ \Psi(x) \;=\; Z(\cdot)\,W \;+\; \varepsilon \] (8)

\(Z\) known basis functions (RBF / Fourier)  ·  \(W\) unknown weights, \(\|W\|\le W_{\max}\)  ·  \(\|\varepsilon\|\le E\).

Control law & the one knob  (10–11) \[ u = Z\,\breve W + \dot x_d - Ke, \qquad \breve W = \underbrace{\hat W}_{\textcolor{#5B9BFF}{\text{stability}}} + \underbrace{\delta W}_{\textcolor{#FEC526}{\text{safety}}} \]
One vector holds everything the robot doesn’t know, and \(\delta W\) is the only safety knob.
Our approach works on any control-affine nonlinear system, including robots. We use a regressor-based representation of the system, where a function approximator and an unknown parameter vector encode our model. Our control law consists of an inverse dynamics term, a proportional error term, and the desired trajectory; the parameter update has two parts, one coming from Lyapunov analysis and one from an online optimization problem.
Main Result  ·  Proposition 1 & Theorem 2

Vanishing conservativeness via a decaying parameter-error bound

Proposition 1: the certificate \[ \|\tilde W(t)\| \;\le\; \sqrt{\dfrac{\,2\bar V_0 \;-\; k_e\displaystyle\int_0^{t}\!\|e(\tau)\|^{2}\,d\tau\,}{\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma^{-1})}} \;=\; \bar W(t) \] (16)

Every term of \(\bar V_0\) is known a priori, and the integral only grows, so \(\bar W(t)\) is non-increasing. Stability is never traded away (Thm. 1); \(\hat W\) need not converge (Remark 1).

One QP on the update law  (18), (20)  ·  OSQP, 100 Hz \[ \begin{aligned} \min_{\delta W}\;& \tfrac12\,\delta W^{\!\top}\delta W \qquad \text{s.t.}\quad \text{RaCLF (15)} \;\;\textcolor{#5B9BFF}{\text{keeps stability}} \\[2pt] & \nabla h\,Z\,\delta W \;\ge\; -\alpha\big(h(x)\big) + \nabla h\,(Ke-\dot x_d) + \underbrace{\|\nabla h Z\|\,\bar W(t)}_{\textcolor{#FF6B6B}{\text{model error, shrinks}}} + \|\nabla h\|\,E \end{aligned} \]
As \(\bar W(t)\) decays, the constraint relaxes itself.
A nice characteristic of our method is its vanishing conservativeness. We start the safety filter with a conservative, worst-case estimation error, and thanks to the time-decaying error bound we derived, the safety filter relaxes over time and allows the robot to use its full potential. Our QP is shown here; it can be solved in a few milliseconds and generates safe, stabilizing parameter vectors.

We also validate the framework in hardware. It solves a quadrupedal navigation problem, where we have an inaccurate locomotion controller with some unknown drift, and our controller successfully takes the robot to the goal pose.
Conclusion

Put the barrier where the uncertainty lives.

1 Safety on the update law, not the output. A CBF-QP perturbs \(\delta W\) so the parameter estimate itself is always safe, with no external filter.
2 Stability is never sacrificed. The RaCLF constraint holds for every \(\delta W\) the QP may choose (Thm. 1).
3 Conservativeness vanishes. A certified, monotonically decreasing bound \(\bar W(t)\) on the parameter error relaxes the CBF as the robot learns (Prop. 1).

Validated on a nonlinear mass–damper, a drifting mobile robot, a 6-DoF UR10 in IsaacSim, and a Unitree Go1 in hardware, all with fully unknown dynamics.
